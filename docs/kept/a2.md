# A2 notes

Background work is a persistent job queue in `library.sqlite`, a worker that runs it in a separate low-priority process, and a policy that puts playback first.

**Queue** (`desktop/job-queue.js`, one migration appended to `catalog-store.js`). Table `jobs`: class, subject, JSON payload, state (`queued`, `leased`, `done`, `failed`), priority (higher first), attempts and max attempts, `run_after`, lease owner and expiry, checkpoint, result, last error. One row per class and subject, so enqueueing again is idempotent: a queued or leased job is returned as it is, a done or failed one starts over with the new payload. `claimJobs` leases due jobs and also jobs whose lease has expired, which is how work resumes after a crash with its last checkpoint. A job that keeps losing its worker (attempts used up by expired leases) is failed, not claimed forever. `failJob` retries after 1 s, 2 s, 4 s … up to 60 s, then stays `failed` (`retryFailedJobs` revives them). A worker that lost its lease cannot renew, complete or fail the job. `recoverJobs` runs at start: the profile's single-instance lock means every lease on disk belongs to a dead worker. A clean stop gives the attempt back (`releaseJob`). All times come from the caller, so the queue is tested with a fake clock.

**Worker** (`desktop/library-worker.js`) runs in the main process but only coordinates: it claims up to the policy's concurrency, renews leases every third of the lease (30 s), stores checkpoints, runs the class's `commit` and completes or fails the job. Handlers run in a **utility process** (`desktop/library-process.js`, `library-process-child.js`). Chosen over a worker thread because it is a separate OS process: `os.setPriority(0, 10)` lowers its priority (a thread cannot), a crash or a leak takes down only the handler, and B1's onnxruntime-web WASM host can be given a process of its own. A process dies and is replaced for the next job; its jobs fail and retry. `library.sqlite` stays owned by the one catalog thread: the worker process never opens it, results come back as messages.

**Adding a job class.** Write `desktop/jobs/<name>-job.js` with `export default async ctx => result`, where `ctx.payload`, `ctx.checkpoint` (what the last attempt saved), `ctx.attempts`, `ctx.signal`, `ctx.save(data)` (a checkpoint) and `ctx.rest()` (await it between small slices of work: it waits during a hold and spaces the work while playing). Then add `{ name, process, module, maxAttempts, commit }` to `desktop/library-job-classes.js`. `process` names the process: classes with the same name share one, a heavy class (model inference) takes its own name and so its own process. `commit(job, result, store)` runs in the main process just before the job is completed, to put the result where Kept reads it (hashing writes `file_hashes`). Enqueue with `worker.enqueue([{ class, subject, payload, priority? }])` and read results with `worker.waitFor(id)`. The handler module is loaded with `import()` in the worker process on first use, so heavy dependencies load only there. Checkpoints must be JSON; the `hash` class has none because a SHA-256 state cannot be saved, and a file is short.

**Hashing** is the `hash` class (`desktop/jobs/hash-job.js`, subject `<source>:<path>`). `ingestLibrary` lists the files that changed and `prefetchHashes` enqueues them in chunks of 500; the loop then reads each result in its order (`desktop/hash-jobs.js`). The job's `commit` stores the hash in `file_hashes` before the job is done, so after a crash the scanner finds the hash and reads nothing. Jobs left in the queue at quit continue at the next launch, before the music server is up. The A1a thread worker (`audio-hasher.js`) is gone.

**Policy** (`desktop/resource-policy.js`). The renderer reports its audio element over the bridge (`window.musicJobs.reportPlayback`, `frontend/src/lib/playback-activity.ts`): `state { playing, waiting }` when it changes, `track-start` on `loadstart`, `stall` on `waiting` or `stalled`. The main process turns it into a snapshot:

| Mode | When | Concurrency | Duty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| idle | paused, or no window | 2 | 100 % |
| playing | the element is playing | 1 | 30 % (work, then rest 2.3× as long, at most 500 ms) |
| hold | 5 s after a track starts, 2 s after play resumes or the audio recovers, 8 s after a stall, and for as long as the element is waiting | 0 | none: running handlers wait at their next `ctx.rest()`, no job is claimed |

A window that reloads or dies reports "not playing". `KEPT_JOBS_POLICY=off` disables the policy to measure it (the process keeps its low priority).

**Progress.** `window.musicJobs.status()` and `onChange` (pushed at most every 200 ms) give `{ mode, classes: { hash: { queued, running, done, failed } } }` since the queue was last empty. `Grid.svelte` shows it ("reading files 312 / 951") in the existing fixed-position indexing pill, so it adds no layout.

**Measured** on an Apple Silicon laptop, internal SSD, with the real Electron app (a temporary profile, the bundled Navidrome, a private real collection only read, 958 songs), driven over the DevTools protocol: first album played through Kept's own player (muted, so nothing is heard), next track every 25 s, the window's `waiting`, `stalled` and `error` events counted, a 10 ms timer in the renderer and in the main process for event-loop lag, and the audio clock sampled every 250 ms for gaps. Hashing started by itself after the scan and ran at the same time as playback. Two runs each, same machine, other work going on in the background (so the maxima are noisy).

| Policy | Hash all 958 files | `waiting` / `stalled` / audio errors | Audio clock gaps over 0.3 s | Renderer lag p95 / max | Main lag p95 / max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| on | 83.5 s and 82 s | 3 / 0 / 0 and 3 / 0 / 0 (the 3 are the initial buffering of 3 tracks) | 0 and 0 | 2.5 / 9 ms and 1.5 / 47.9 ms | 4 / 19 ms and 4 / 54 ms |
| off | 17.3 s and 17 s | 0 / 0 / 0 and 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 and 0 | 2.5 / 49.6 ms and 2.6 / 43.4 ms | 3 / 43 ms and 3 / 33 ms |
| no jobs | | 0 or 1 waiting per track load, 0 stalled | 0 | 1.4 to 2.6 / 3 to 15 ms | 3 to 4 / 11 to 22 ms |

What it says. On a fast SSD with a stream from the local server, hashing at full speed did not stall or glitch the audio at all, and event-loop lag was the same with and without the policy within the noise (the one clear gap, a 9 ms and 19 ms maximum, did not repeat in the second run). So this machine does not show the policy protecting playback, and it makes hashing about 5 times slower (83 s instead of 17 s, the library fully hashed with the music playing). It is the right default for a slow or busy disk, a network drive or a phone, where hashing and playback do compete, and none of those was measured: **a USB or network drive, a small laptop on battery and a phone are untested**, as is audible playback (the element was muted) and the first ten seconds of a cold-cache library. If a slower profile turns out to matter, the knobs are `PLAYING_DUTY` and the hold times in `resource-policy.js`.

Tests: `job-queue.test.js` (lease expiry and re-claim, checkpoint resume after a restart on a reopened file, idempotent enqueue, retry and backoff, poison jobs, clean release), `resource-policy.test.js` (every mode transition and the gate, with a fake clock), `library-worker.test.js` (the worker with a hand-moved clock, lost leases, stop; and a real worker process: result and nice value, a throwing handler, a process that dies, resume from a checkpoint after the death, work held during a stall), `catalog-ingest.test.js` (the sync hashes through the job queue in a real process), `jobs-activity.svelte.test.ts` (what the window reports).

Open: the progress text is not checked at the four audit viewports; a window that stops reporting while playing (a hung renderer) keeps the policy throttled, which is the safe direction; the `hash` job is one file, so a very large library enqueues tens of thousands of rows (chunks of 500, and finished rows are pruned after an hour).
