# B0 findings

### Pass bar (set before the run)

The spike lives in `spikes/sound/`. The bar below was written before any neighbour
list or score was computed.

- **Blind sheet.** 20 seed albums, chosen deterministically and spread over genre
  tags. Each seed gets two lists of 5 albums, both excluding the seed's artist:
  sound-nearest, and same genre tag. The lists are shown shuffled as X and Y. The
  user picks the better list per seed; a tie counts for neither. Sound passes when
  it is preferred in at least 13 of 20 seeds.
- **Automatic proxy.** Both must hold:
  1. Held-out track retrieval: for each track, the nearest other track in the
     collection comes from the same album at least 5 times as often as chance.
  2. Genre agreement: the top-5 sound neighbours of an album (excluding its
     artist) share its genre tag at least 1.5 times as often as chance, with
     p < 0.01 on a 1,000-shuffle permutation test.
- **Representation.** Signal-processing features, OpenL3, or both combined: the
  one with the best held-out track retrieval is used for the sheet, chosen before
  any list is looked at.
- **Mood.** Tested only with at least 30 hand-tagged albums that have analysed
  audio. An axis counts as learnable when cross-validated error is at least 15%
  below predicting the mean.
- **Cost.** Seconds per track for decoding, features and embedding, and peak
  memory, measured in one single-threaded process.

### Result

Run on the real collection on an Apple M1 Max: 958 audio files, 942 analysed
(16 loose files held no decodable audio). Grouping gave 55 albums of 3 or more
tracks, 833 tracks and 47 artists. Per track: three 10 s excerpts at 20%, 50% and
80%, 15 one-second OpenL3 windows.

**Verdict: fails the bar by one seed.** The user judged the blind sheet on
2026-11-19: sound preferred in 12 of 20 seeds, tags (mostly same-folder picks) in
7, one tie. The bar was 13, so sound-only similarity does not ship as written.
Of the 19 decided seeds sound won 63%, which is a lean, not proof (p ≈ 0.18).

**Rethink.** B1 still builds the analyser and feature store, because Dig's
computed axes need them. B2 changes: "More like this" becomes a hybrid of sound
and the user's own structure (folder, artist, listening history), and must win a
second blind sheet against the same-folder baseline before it ships. The
estimated energy, acoustic and vocal values must pass their own check: the user
marks whether they look right for 20 albums, and an axis ships only when at least
14 of 20 look right. An axis that fails stays hand-set only.

Earlier status line: undecided until the blind sheet is judged. Proxy 1 passes clearly.
Proxy 2 cannot be evaluated as written, because the collection has almost no genre
tags. Generate the sheet with `pnpm evaluate` in `spikes/sound/`; it is written to
`spikes/sound/out/blind-judging.html`.

| Proxy 1: nearest other track is from the same album | Share | Lift over chance (3.8%) | Same artist, own album removed (chance 7.4%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal-processing features (42 values) | 64.5% | 17× | 33% |
| OpenL3 (512 values) | 82.2% | 22× | 51% |
| Both combined (used for the sheet) | 83.3% | 22× | 49% |

- **Proxy 1 passes** (bar: 5×). The same-artist column removes the track's own
  album, so shared mastering does not explain it: OpenL3 finds the same artist on
  another album 7 times as often as chance (192 tracks).
- **Proxy 2 fails as written, for lack of data.** Only 10 of 55 albums carry a
  genre tag, with 11 distinct tags. On those 10, neighbours share a tag 8% of the
  time against 1.8% by chance, p = 0.025. A substitute chosen after the fact, the
  two top-level folders the user sorted the collection into, gives 81% agreement
  against 52% by chance (1.55×, p = 0.001); OpenL3 alone gives 85% (1.64×).
- **The genre baseline barely exists on this collection.** The baseline list had a
  genre-tag match for 1 of 20 seeds. The other slots were filled from the same
  top-level folder in a fixed hash order. This departs from the bar: the sheet
  mostly compares sound with an arbitrary pick from the same folder. Seeds were
  spread over the folders (13 and 7), one per artist. The two lists share 0.7 of
  5 albums on average.
- **Mood: not testable.** The three app profiles hold 0 hand-set Dig traits (the
  catalog stores are empty). The test harness is ready (`pnpm mood`); its
  self-check recovers a planted signal (71% below the mean baseline) and rejects
  noise (1%). The energy, acoustic and vocal proxies are heuristics with nothing
  to validate them against; they appear on the sheet after the reveal.

| Cost per track, one single-threaded process (39 tracks) | Seconds |
| --- | --- |
| Tags and duration (ffprobe) | 0.08 |
| Decode three excerpts (ffmpeg) | 0.22 |
| Signal-processing features | 0.21 |
| OpenL3 mel frontend | 0.14 |
| OpenL3 inference, 15 windows at 0.57 s | 8.33 |
| Total | 9.0 |

Peak memory is 481 MB for the Node process and 25 MB for ffmpeg. The model loads
in 0.2 s. The whole collection takes about 2.4 hours on one thread (23 minutes on
8 processes); a 10,000-track library would take about 25 hours.

**Model.** OpenL3 mel256, music, 512 dimensions: code MIT, weights CC BY 4.0
(`marl/openl3`, Cramer et al. 2019). No public ONNX export exists. The spike
exports the PyTorch port `torchopenl3_mel256_music_512.pth.tar` from
`torchopenl3/torchopenl3-models` (Apache-2.0 repository) to an 18.7 MB ONNX file
and reimplements the mel frontend in JavaScript. Against the PyTorch reference
the embedding differs by at most 8e-5. Attribution for the CC BY weights belongs
on the third-party notices screen.

**For B1.**
- Use OpenL3 as the similarity signal. The signal-processing features add 1 point
  to retrieval; keep them only as inputs for the Dig axes.
- Inference is 93% of the cost. Before building B1, measure retrieval with 6
  windows per track and with the mel128 model; each should cut the time by half
  or more.
- Do not rely on genre tags as a fallback or as a check: plan for libraries where
  they are absent.
- Keep the mood axis held. Re-run `pnpm mood` once 30 albums are hand-tagged.

### Cheaper OpenL3 settings

Same 833 album tracks, OpenL3 alone, scored with `node src/variants.js`. Seconds
are for OpenL3 only (mel frontend and inference) in one process, on the same 39
tracks as before; decoding and the signal-processing features add 0.5 s.

| Setting | Same album | Same artist, own album removed | OpenL3 s per track | Peak memory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mel256, 15 windows (round 1) | 82.2% | 51% | 8.5 | 481 MB |
| mel256, 6 windows | 78.6% | 45% | 3.4 | 523 MB |
| mel128, 15 windows | 80.2% | 34% | 4.3 | 517 MB |
| mel128, 6 windows | 77.6% | 34% | 1.7 | 521 MB |
| mel256, 15 windows, 2 threads | as 1 thread | as 1 thread | 4.3 | 526 MB |
| mel128, 6 windows, 2 threads | as 1 thread | as 1 thread | 0.9 | 521 MB |

- **Use mel256.** mel128 halves the time but drops the same-artist check from 51%
  to 34%: it leans on the album's own sound more than on the music. That check
  covers 192 tracks, so read it as a direction, not a precise figure.
- **6 windows cost 3.6 points** on the same-album check and 5 on the same-artist
  check, for 2.5 times the speed. A 10,000-track library takes about 11 hours on
  one thread instead of 25.
- **Two threads halve the wall time** with the same output. They spend the same
  processor time, so they suit an idle machine, not playback.
- **Memory does not depend on the setting.** About 440 MB is taken when the
  session is created, with either model and with graph optimisation off. B1 should
  find out why before accepting it. The differences in the table are noise.
- **mel128 weights** have the same licence as mel256: CC BY 4.0, stated for all
  model weights in the `marl/openl3` README (SHA-256 of the port used:
  `4363d115…684adc7d`).

### Round 2 bar (set before generating)

Written before any round 2 list was built, after reading the round 1 result.

- **What went wrong in round 1.** The 12 sound wins had 0.08 albums from the
  other top-level folder per list; the 7 losses had 2.3, and 6 of the 7 had at
  least one. The fifth neighbour was also weaker in the losses (similarity 0.40
  against 0.55). Sound alone mixes the two halves of the collection that the user
  keeps apart.
- **Hybrid.** Score = sound similarity + w when two albums share a top-level
  folder. w is tuned only on proxy 1 (the nearest other track is from the same
  album, with the bonus applied between tracks), never on the judged seeds. The
  smallest w that reaches the best proxy 1 value is used.
- **Play history is not used.** The app profiles hold 60 local plays, all from
  one 13-minute stretch on one day, so there is no listening pattern to learn.
- **Sheet 2.** 20 new seeds: none of the round 1 seed albums or their artists,
  spread over the folders, one per artist. Hybrid top 5 against the same-folder
  baseline of round 1, both excluding the seed's artist, sides balanced. The
  hybrid passes when it is preferred in at least 13 of 20 seeds.
- **Traits check.** 20 albums spread over the folders, one per artist. Each shows
  the estimated energy, acoustic and vocal value as a word on a five-step scale
  relative to the collection. The estimates are the round 1 heuristics, unchanged.
  An axis passes when at least 14 of 20 look right.

### Round 2 sheets

Generated with `node src/round2.js` in `spikes/sound/`; not judged yet.

- **Folder weight.** Proxy 1 rises from 83.3% with no bonus to 84.3% at w = 0.3
  and stays there up to w = 1, so w = 0.3 is used, on the combined features. The
  gain on the proxy is small: the folder mostly matters between albums, which
  proxy 1 does not measure. With OpenL3 alone the same-artist check rises from
  51% to 56%.
- **Sheet 2** (`out/blind-judging-2.html`): 20 new seeds, 14 and 6 over the two
  folders, none shared with round 1. Hybrid lists hold 0.2 albums from the other
  folder on average, against 1 in round 1, and differ from the sound-only list by
  0.9 albums of 5. They share 1 album of 5 with the baseline.
- **Traits check** (`out/traits-check.html`): 20 albums, three estimates each,
  with "looks right", "looks wrong" and "not sure". "Not sure" does not count as
  right. Expect energy to do best: the acoustic estimate tracks quietness more
  than instrumentation, and the vocal estimate rests on one weak cue.

### Traits check result (2026-11-19)

The user judged 20 albums. Energy looked right for 15 of 20 and passes. Acoustic
looked right for 11 (one unsure) and vocal for 13, so both fail the bar of 14.
B2 ships estimated energy only. Acoustic and vocal stay hand-set only until a
better estimate passes the same check.

### Crowd reference

An automatic check against crowdsourced data, next to the user's own blind judgement. `pnpm crowd-fetch` (MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz) and `pnpm crowd-lastfm` cache responses in `out/crowd/` (gitignored; only artist and album names are sent, generic User-Agent; the Last.fm key is read from `.env` at runtime); `pnpm crowd-eval` scores. No weight was tuned on these references.

**Licences.** ListenBrainz data, including the Labs similar-artists endpoint, is stated as CC0 and available for commercial use (MetaBrainz asks commercial users to support it); no source names the Labs endpoint's terms itself, so confirm with MetaBrainz before shipping. MusicBrainz core data is CC0, but tags and genres are CC BY-NC-SA: evaluation only. Last.fm terms rule out shipping: evaluation only.

**Coverage is thin.** 30 of 46 artists matched an MBID (score at least 95 and exact name); 22 of 55 albums matched a release group. Last.fm found similar lists for 35 of 46 artists, artist tags for 28, album tags for 9 of 55 albums. Albums with a usable reference: 4 (ListenBrainz similar), 19 (MB genre), 20 (Last.fm similar). The collection is largely small or regional artists.

| Precision@5, seed's artist excluded | MB shared genre (19) | Last.fm similar (20) | ListenBrainz similar (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 15.6% | 2.4% | 1.9% |
| Same folder | 25.3% (1.6x) | 0% (0x) | 5% |
| Sound, both (round 1 sheet) | 30.5% (2.0x, CI 1.4-2.5) | 8% (3.3x, CI 1.0-5.1) | 10% |
| Sound, OpenL3 only | 36.8% (2.4x, CI 1.8-3.0) | 5% (2.1x, CI 0.7-3.6) | 5% |
| Hybrid (both + folder, w 0.3) | 31.6% (2.0x, CI 1.5-2.5) | 3% (1.2x, CI 0-2.7) | 10% |

Lift CIs are bootstrap 95% over albums. The ListenBrainz column (4 albums) is anecdote.

**Traits (Last.fm tags, count at least 15, artist plus album).** Labels are too sparse: acoustic has 8 acoustic against 5 electronic albums, vocal 2 against 6, energy 2 against 2, so only acoustic can be tested. The heuristic acoustic estimate scores AUC 0.57 (CI 0.13-1.00), close to chance. A kernel ridge on album vectors trained on those tags, leaving the held-out artist out, scores 0.72 (CI 0.38-1.00) with DSP, OpenL3 or both. That points the right way but 13 albums cannot establish it. A better acoustic estimate looks plausible; vocal and energy cannot be judged from tags here. Hand labels from the traits check (20 albums, plus more) are the better training set. Not shipped.

**Verdict.** Both genre and Last.fm similar-artist references lean the same way as the user: sound lists beat same-folder lists (2.0x against 1.6x on genre; 3.3x against 0 on Last.fm), with the folder bonus adding nothing and on Last.fm hurting. Intervals are wide and the references reach 19 to 20 albums, so this agrees with the 12 against 7 lean without proving it. ListenBrainz covers 4 albums and cannot add to it. As an online source for "More like this" in B2 it is licence-friendly but this library is too niche for it to help; it suits mainstream libraries, behind consent, as an optional boost that falls back to sound. Last.fm cannot ship.

### B2 decision (2026-11-19)

The user's round-1 lean (sound 12, same folder 7) and both crowd references
(MusicBrainz genres, Last.fm similar artists) favour sound over same-folder lists,
and the folder bonus did not help on either reference. The user chose to ship
"More like this" from sound similarity only, labelled as sound-based. The folder
bonus is set to 0. The user then dropped the second blind sheet; it was never
judged and plays no part in the decision.
